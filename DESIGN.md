# مُسْتَحَقّ Brand Identity

## Brand Foundation

مُسْتَحَقّ means money that has been earned and is due. The brand should make freelancers and small business owners feel that their work is legitimate, their agreements are documented, and their cash flow is finally visible.

The target audience is practical rather than corporate: Egyptian and MENA freelancers, consultants, designers, developers, marketers, agency owners, and shop owners who currently manage income through WhatsApp, Excel, memory, and scattered payment apps. The interface should feel professional enough to send to a client, but not cold enough to feel like enterprise banking software.

The visual identity is **Earned clarity**: warm paper-like surfaces for contracts and onboarding, deep ink for trust, Nile teal for action and verification, and clear semantic colors for paid, pending, overdue, and destructive states.

## Voice and Visual Personality

Use calm confidence. Copy should be direct, useful, and lightly reassuring. Avoid startup hype, vague AI promises, or overly playful finance language. This product touches money, contracts, tax estimates, and client payments, so every screen should make the next action feel clear and low-risk.

The product should feel:

- **Trustworthy:** banking-grade contrast, stable layouts, predictable interactions.
- **Human:** warm backgrounds, rounded document cards, Arabic-first typography.
- **Efficient:** dense enough for dashboards, generous enough for onboarding.
- **Protective:** overdue, renewal, tax, and subscription warnings are clear without being alarmist.

## Logo and Wordmark

The wordmark should prioritize the Arabic name `مُسْتَحَقّ`. Use the Latin `Mustahaq` as a secondary label when useful for bilingual contexts, payment links, exports, and app metadata.

Recommended mark direction: a simple geometric monogram or seal built from a ledger line, check mark, and Arabic dot rhythm. It should work as a favicon, app icon, PDF stamp, and payment receipt mark.

Logo usage rules:

- Keep clear space equal to the height of the Arabic letter body around the mark.
- Use `mustahaqInk` on light backgrounds and `papyrus` or white on dark backgrounds.
- Do not add gradients, glows, stretched outlines, or emoji-like symbols.
- Minimum digital wordmark width: `96px`; icon-only mark minimum: `24px`.

## Color System

The palette maps finance trust to MENA warmth. Deep navy ink anchors the interface, teal carries primary action, papyrus and sand give contracts and onboarding a tactile paper quality, and gold signals attention without using red for everything.

Primary palette:

- `mustahaqInk` `#102A43`: authority, text, dark surfaces.
- `nileTeal` `#0F766E`: primary CTAs, active navigation, verified payments.
- `papyrus` `#F7F2E8`: marketing backgrounds, contract previews, onboarding panels.
- `sand` `#E8DDC7`: warm borders, secondary fills, document dividers.
- `dateGold` `#D99A2B`: pending payments, renewal reminders, highlighted insights.
- `profitMint` `#16A34A`: paid, healthy cash flow, savings.
- `coralDebt` `#DC4A3D`: overdue, destructive actions, serious errors.
- `cloud` `#F9FAF8`: default app canvas.
- `slate` `#52616B`: secondary copy and metadata.

Semantic color rules:

- Use teal for the one primary action on a screen.
- Use green only for confirmed positive financial states, not general decoration.
- Use gold for pending, upcoming, or review-needed states.
- Use coral-red only for overdue, destructive, failed, or dangerous states.
- Never communicate payment status by color alone. Pair color with labels, icons, or both.

## Typography

The brand should use a two-font Arabic-first system:

- **Display:** `Noto Kufi Arabic`, 700-800. Use for hero headlines, section titles, wordmark, and high-confidence screen headings.
- **Body/UI:** `IBM Plex Sans Arabic`, 400-700. Use for forms, dashboard content, tables, captions, and dense product surfaces.
- **Latin companion:** `IBM Plex Sans`, aligned with the body family for English labels, URLs, amounts, and exports.

This pairing avoids generic startup typography while staying highly readable for Arabic financial workflows. Use tabular numbers for balances, tax estimates, invoice totals, due dates, and chart labels.

Hierarchy:

- Hero: `clamp(2.5rem, 5.5vw, 5rem)`, 800, tight but readable.
- Section title: `clamp(1.875rem, 3vw, 2.5rem)`, 700.
- Card title: `1rem`, 700, display family for product modules.
- Body: `1rem`, 400, `1.6rem` line-height.
- Caption: `0.75rem`, 500, for metadata and timestamps.
- Financial figure: `clamp(1.75rem, 3vw, 3rem)`, 700, tabular numbers.

## Layout and Spacing

Use an 8px rhythm for page composition and a 4px token for fine component alignment. The app should balance two modes: onboarding and marketing need breathing room; dashboards need scannable density.

Layout principles:

- RTL is the default. Primary reading flow, sidebar placement, field alignment, icon placement, and directional arrows should respect Arabic-first behavior.
- Dashboard pages use a strong top summary row, then cards for payment links, contracts, income, subscriptions, and alerts.
- Contract and payment-link surfaces should look more like documents than generic SaaS cards.
- Empty states should include a direct next action, since the PRD emphasizes time-to-value under 90 seconds.
- Avoid perfectly generic three-card layouts on marketing pages. Use asymmetric document/payment previews to make the product memorable.

Spacing guidance:

- Mobile page gutter: `1rem`.
- Desktop page gutter: `2rem`.
- Marketing section vertical padding: `6rem`.
- Dashboard grid gap: `1rem`.
- Form section gap: `1.5rem`.
- Minimum touch target: `2.75rem`, especially for mobile auth and onboarding.

## Components

Primary buttons use `nileTeal` with light text. They should feel confident, not loud. Use one primary CTA per view: create payment link, create contract, connect Gmail, or continue onboarding.

Secondary actions use warm neutral fills or outlines. Destructive actions are reserved for cancellation, deletion, disconnecting accounts, and overdue remediation.

Cards should follow the user's mental model:

- **Payment link cards:** amount, status, client, due date, share action.
- **Contract cards:** document-like surface, milestone progress, client acceptance status.
- **Income cards:** large financial figure, trend, source breakdown.
- **Expense cards:** service identity, renewal date, amount, cancel link state.
- **AI review cards:** extracted data, confidence, approve/reject controls.

Inputs should feel stable and clear. Use visible labels, helper text for financial implications, and explicit currency/tax suffixes. Never hide important financial context inside placeholders.

## Data Visualization

Charts should clarify cash flow, not decorate the page. Use the chart palette in a consistent order:

1. `nileTeal` for income or primary series.
2. `profitMint` for paid or healthy states.
3. `dateGold` for pending or upcoming payments.
4. `coralDebt` for overdue or risk.
5. `mustahaqInk` or blue variant for neutral comparison.

Use direct labels where possible. Avoid unexplained legends on small screens. Currency values should always show currency code or symbol consistently, because the product supports EGP and USD.

## Motion and Interaction

Motion should make the system feel responsive and orderly. Use short color, opacity, and shadow transitions for daily dashboard interactions. Reserve larger page-load choreography for the landing page and onboarding completion.

Interaction rules:

- Prefer color, opacity, and shadow changes over scale transforms that shift layout.
- Use progress motion for onboarding and email scanning, but provide clear text status.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by disabling decorative movement.
- Loading states for financial operations should name the operation, such as "Creating payment link" or "Scanning subscriptions", instead of showing a generic spinner alone.

## Accessibility

The identity must preserve trust through accessibility:

- Normal text contrast should meet at least `4.5:1`.
- Large text and icons should meet at least `3:1`.
- Focus rings use teal with enough contrast on both light and dark backgrounds.
- All payment, contract, and subscription states need text labels, not color alone.
- Forms must support keyboard navigation and clear error recovery.
- Arabic diacritics in the brand name should render correctly, but body copy should not overuse diacritics where they reduce scan speed.

## Marketing Direction

The landing page should sell relief from scattered financial work: WhatsApp links, Excel chaos, forgotten subscriptions, late clients, and unclear tax exposure. Visuals should show the product bringing these fragments into one orderly workspace.

Recommended hero composition:

- Warm papyrus background with a faint ledger or geometric grid.
- Large Arabic headline in `Noto Kufi Arabic`.
- Product preview cards for payment link, milestone contract, income summary, and subscription scanner.
- One primary CTA in teal and one secondary CTA as outline.
- Trust cues for Arabic-first, local payment methods, Gmail scanning, and no company registration required for the MVP path.

Avoid generic AI gradients, floating purple blobs, emoji icons, and stock business illustrations. The AI feature should feel like a quiet assistant that finds money leaks, not a separate sci-fi product.

## Slides and Documents

For pitch decks, reports, PDFs, and contract exports:

- Slide background: `papyrus` for narrative slides, `mustahaqInk` for section dividers.
- Slide titles: `Noto Kufi Arabic`, 36-48pt, `mustahaqInk` or `papyrus`.
- Body text: `IBM Plex Sans Arabic`, 18-24pt.
- Charts: cycle `nileTeal`, `profitMint`, `dateGold`, `coralDebt`, then muted navy.
- Contract PDFs: warm paper background, ink text, teal signature/accepted stamp, clear tax and total breakdowns.
- Receipts: high contrast, minimal decoration, prominent paid status, downloadable bilingual metadata.

## Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- Purple AI gradients or generic white SaaS screens.
- Inter, Roboto, Arial, or system-font-first branding.
- Emoji as icons.
- Red for every alert or unresolved item.
- Hiding fees, tax, due dates, or renewal timing in low-contrast metadata.
- Dense dashboards without a first action.
- Marketing claims that imply legal, tax, or escrow guarantees the MVP does not provide.

## Implementation Notes

The current application uses shadcn-style CSS variables and Tailwind. When applying this identity to code, update `resources/css/app.css` semantic tokens first, then component-specific styling. Keep tokens semantic, not page-specific, so dashboards, auth, marketing, and exported documents share the same system.

Font loading should add `Noto Kufi Arabic`, `IBM Plex Sans Arabic`, and `IBM Plex Sans`. If loading all three is too expensive during the hackathon, use `IBM Plex Sans Arabic` for all UI text and keep `Noto Kufi Arabic` only for marketing headings and the wordmark.
