## Brand and style

The product presents itself as a calm, trustworthy workspace for independent professionals and small businesses. The visual personality is restrained and modern: almost everything reads as neutral graphite and off-white in light mode, with a clean inversion in dark mode so primary actions become light-on-dark instead of dark-on-light. There is no loud brand accent hue in the core interface; emphasis comes from weight, contrast, and layout rather than from saturated marketing colors.

The Arabic-first experience uses a humanist sans with clear counters and strong weight steps, so headings can carry energy while body copy stays readable for long sessions. The marketing surface adds a subtle technical texture—a faint square grid behind the hero—suggesting structure and control without looking like a spreadsheet.

## Colors

Semantic colors are defined in OKLCH with near-zero chroma for neutrals, so backgrounds and borders feel clean and paper-like. Primary and foreground swap roles between themes: in light mode the user reads dark type on white; in dark mode, light type on deep neutral surfaces. Secondary, muted, and accent fills are soft steps off the base so hover states feel like a gentle tint shift, not a new color family.

Destructive states use a warm red in OKLCH for errors and dangerous actions. Chart visualization uses five distinct hues in OKLCH reserved for data series so dashboards stay interpretable without borrowing semantic reds and greens from alerts.

Sidebar tokens mirror the main surface system but allow the rail to sit slightly off the main canvas tone for hierarchy. Marketing-only lines use a very light gray at low alpha for grid guides so they never compete with text.

## Typography

Cairo is the sole UI and marketing typeface, loaded in weights from regular through extrabold. Marketing heroes use the heaviest weight and tight tracking for impact; in-app titles favor semibold at default text sizes. Muted foreground color scales down supporting sentences and descriptions. Form labels stay at small or body-small size with medium weight; buttons use small text with medium weight for a compact control bar.

Line-length is controlled in marketing with max-width containers; in forms, fields stack with consistent vertical rhythm. Because the layout is RTL-first, alignment, padding, and “end” emphasis follow right-to-left logic in copy and controls.

## Layout and spacing

Spacing follows a four-pixel step system: one unit equals one quarter of a rem. Cards and auth-style forms use generous horizontal padding and a one-and-a-half-rem vertical rhythm between blocks. Dashboard-style regions use tighter tile gaps for dense monitoring, while marketing sections use much larger vertical padding so the page breathes.

The shell layout pairs a persistent navigation rail with a main inset that fills the viewport height. On small viewports the rail becomes a sheet with a slightly wider width than the desktop rail for touch comfort. Content areas often use rounded outer corners and a light shadow where the inset meets the window edge, reinforcing that the main workspace is a single sheet.

## Elevation and depth

Depth is understated. Cards and inputs use a minimal shadow or a hairline border—or both—so surfaces feel tactile but not glossy. Primary buttons add an extra-small shadow to separate them from flat backgrounds. Dropdowns, dialogs, and similar overlays step up to medium or large shadows so stacking order is obvious.

Modal overlays use a dark scrim at high opacity so focus stays on the dialog. The sticky marketing header can sit over content with a slight transparency and backdrop blur so the transition feels continuous rather than harsh.

## Shapes

Corner radii are built from a single base radius token and derived smaller steps for nested controls. Cards favor extra-large rounding; default chips, inputs, and sidebar entries use medium rounding relative to that base. Marketing feature cards use a larger radius than standard dashboard cards for a friendlier, brochure-like silhouette. Checkboxes use a small fixed radius for a crisp, form-like appearance.

## Motion and interaction

Micro-interactions favor short durations: layout chrome such as the sidebar animates over a few hundred milliseconds with linear easing for predictable mechanical motion. Color and shadow transitions on buttons and inputs avoid bouncy curves so the UI feels precise.

Overlays enter and exit with a short fade combined with a slight scale change so popovers and dialogs feel anchored. Loading states use rotation or a soft pulse on neutral placeholders. Text links animate underline decoration over a slightly longer duration with ease-out so hover feels responsive but not twitchy. Marketing cards lift slightly and gain shadow on hover to invite exploration.

## Components

Primary actions are filled neutrals with inverted foreground; outline actions sit on the base background with a neutral border. Ghost actions only show background on hover to reduce noise in toolbars.

Standard cards group related content with internal gap between header and body; titles are semibold with optional description in muted small text. Marketing cards pair an icon tile—rounded container with a low-opacity primary wash—with a bold feature title and relaxed body copy.

Inputs are full-width, transparent inside, with a neutral border and subtle shadow; focus is communicated with a ring in the semantic ring color at partial opacity rather than a hard outline. The sidebar marks the active route with an accent background and medium weight; group labels are visually quieter than menu items.

Destructive styling applies to dangerous buttons and invalid fields; invalid controls also pick up a ring tinted toward the destructive hue in dark mode more strongly than in light mode for visibility.
